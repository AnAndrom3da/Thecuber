    Welcome to the Daisy

    This is the first step in the beginner method for solving a 3×3 Rubik’s Cube.

    Don’t worry about speed, memorization, or fancy moves yet. This step is all about getting comfortable with the cube.

    What Is the Daisy?

    The Daisy is a simple pattern where the yellow center is on top, and the four white edge pieces sit around it.

    🌼 Think of it like petals around a flower.

    • Yellow center in the middle
    • Four white edge pieces around it
    • The white stickers point sideways, not down

    Important Cube Facts

    • Center pieces never move
    • Edge pieces have two colors
    • Corners have three colors (ignore these for now)

    For the Daisy, you are only looking for white edge pieces.

    How to Hold the Cube

    Hold the cube so the yellow center is on top.

    Keep it there the entire time.

    If the yellow center is on top, you’re doing it right 👍

    Step-by-Step: Making the Daisy

    1. Find a White Edge

    Look for a piece with white and one other color. Make sure it is not a corner.

    2. Bring It to the Top Layer

    Use any turns you want to move that white edge to the top of the cube.

    There are no wrong moves at this stage.

    3. Place It Next to the Yellow Center

    Move the piece so the white sticker is touching the yellow center. The side color does not matter yet.

    4. Repeat

    Do the same thing until all four white edge pieces surround the yellow center.

    What the Finished Daisy Looks Like

    • Yellow center on top
    • Four white edges around it
    • No white edges left anywhere else

    If this matches your cube, you’re ready to move on 🎉

    Common Mistakes

    • Using white corners instead of edges
    • Letting the yellow center leave the top
    • Trying to match side colors too early
    • Thinking the cube looks “worse” than before

    Don’t worry — the cube is supposed to look messy right now.

    Why Do We Make the Daisy?

    The Daisy makes the next step — the White Cross — much easier.

    It helps you learn how edge pieces move without pressure.

    You’re Doing Great

    If this is your first time solving a cube, you’re already making progress. Everyone starts here.

    Go slow. Be patient. Understanding comes first.
