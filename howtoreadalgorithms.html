    A complete beginner-to-intermediate guide to Rubik’s Cube notation

    What Is a Cubing Algorithm?

    A cubing algorithm is a sequence of moves written using standard notation. Each move tells you which face to turn and how to turn it. Algorithms are always read from left to right.

    Cube Orientation

    Before starting an algorithm, always hold the cube correctly:

    • Front (F) facing you
    • Up (U) on top
    • Right (R) on your right

    Many mistakes come from starting with the wrong orientation.

    Basic Face Moves

    • R – Right
    • L – Left
    • U – Up
    • D – Down
    • F – Front
    • B – Back

    A single letter means a 90° clockwise turn when looking directly at that face.

    Clockwise vs Perspective (Very Important)

    Clockwise is always defined from the face’s point of view, not yours.

    This is why moves like B, L, and D can feel reversed. Imagine rotating the cube so you are directly facing that side.

    Move Modifiers

    • ' – Counterclockwise
    • 2 – 180° turn

    R' = Right face counterclockwise

    U2 = Top face twice

    Wide Moves

    Wide moves turn two layers at once:

    • Rw or r – Right + middle layer
    • Uw or u – Top + middle layer
    • Fw or f – Front + middle layer

    Slice Moves

    • M – Middle slice (moves like L')
    • E – Equatorial slice (moves like D')
    • S – Standing slice (moves like F)

    Cube Rotations

    Rotations turn the entire cube:

    • x – Rotate like R
    • y – Rotate like U
    • z – Rotate like F

    Triggers (Mini-Algorithms)

    Many algorithms are built from small reusable patterns called triggers. Recognizing them makes algorithms easier to learn.

    • Sexy Move: R U R' U'
    • Inverse Sexy: U R U' R'
    • Sledgehammer: R' F R F'
    • Hedge: F R' F' R

    Understanding Algorithm Purpose

    Algorithms are designed to move only a few pieces while leaving the rest of the cube unchanged.

    Ask yourself:

    • Which pieces move?
    • Which pieces stay the same?

    Inverses (Undoing an Algorithm)

    Every algorithm has an inverse that undoes it.

    1. Reverse the order
    2. Invert each move

    Algorithm: R U R'

    Inverse: R U' R'

    Setup Moves

    Some algorithms temporarily move pieces into position, run a main algorithm, then undo the setup.

    Setup → Algorithm → Undo Setup

    Mirrored Algorithms

    Many right-handed algorithms have left-handed mirrors.

    Right-handed: R U R'

    Left-handed mirror: L' U' L

    Commutators & Conjugates (Advanced)

    Advanced cubing uses structured patterns to precisely control piece movement.

    [A, B] = A B A' B'

    These are powerful tools for creating and understanding algorithms.

    Finger Tricks & Regrips

    • Most U moves are index-finger flicks
    • Avoid regripping whenever possible
    • Efficiency matters more than raw speed

    Common Mistakes

    • Wrong cube orientation
    • Misreading ' or 2
    • Turning too fast before understanding the move

    Final Advice

    Accuracy comes before speed. Slow, correct practice builds fast solves later. Understanding algorithms makes cubing far more enjoyable.

    Happy cubing 🧩 Keep turning!
